## The test
Is it about one person ("I can't", "I'd rather") or about the project? Does it change what the team must do?

## A personal preference
Acknowledge the person kindly, note it, and make clear the plan stands. We should be aware that someone has that preference, but we go with what makes sense for the business and for everyone else. One person being unable to do something doesn't stop the plan, unless it's something offensive or similar.

There's a difference between someone being offended and someone just not liking something. Someone not liking oranges doesn't mean we won't have oranges at a business lunch for visitors from off site, who might like oranges.

## A real clash with the plan
To rule fast, the owner needs, privately: the decision it clashes with, who raised it, and any other options. Publicly, only something neutral, like "flagged to the owner", without exposing the details.
